# The Beginner's Quick-Start Guide to Learning Piano

This is the beginner-friendly version of our full research article, [A Research-Informed Guide to Learning Piano: How Beginners Should Actually Practice](/articles/beginner-pianist-learning-journey). The original stays available as a detailed knowledge base. This guide covers the same ideas in smaller, faster pieces — each point links straight to its source.

## Two Memory Systems, Not One

Playing piano uses two different kinds of memory:

- **Declarative memory** = facts you can say out loud, like "Cmaj7 is C-E-G-B."
- **Procedural memory** = skills you do, like finding that chord shape without thinking.

You can know a chord on paper and still freeze at the keyboard. The two systems are neurologically separate — amnesic patients can learn motor skills even when they cannot form new conscious memories ([Cohen & Squire, 1980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4464729/); [Squire & Wixted, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6389412/)). Train both.

## Active Recall Beats Re-Reading

Forcing yourself to produce an answer from memory strengthens it far more than re-reading. Students who practiced retrieval retained far more one week later than students who repeatedly studied the same material for the same total time ([Roediger & Karpicke, 2006](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf); [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)). Retrieval even outperformed detailed concept mapping one week later ([Karpicke & Blunt, 2011](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1199327)).

**What to do:** Cover the chord chart and ask, "What notes are in Cmaj7#11?" Produce the answer from memory.

## Spaced Repetition Beats Cramming

Memory fades fastest right after learning. Reviewing just before you forget flattens the forgetting curve ([Ebbinghaus, 1885/1913](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9971077/)). A meta-analysis of 317 studies found that spacing study sessions improves long-term retention across ages and materials ([Cepeda et al., 2006](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)), and the longer you want to remember something, the longer the optimal gap between reviews ([Cepeda et al., 2008](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0kp5q19x/qt0kp5q19x.pdf)).

**What to do:** Twenty minutes a day for six days beats two hours on Saturday.

## Let Anki Handle the Scheduling

Anki combines active recall and spaced repetition automatically. Its modern scheduler, FSRS, models how hard a card is for you, how long you tend to remember it, and how likely you are to recall it right now, then fits those values to your personal review history ([Ye, 2022](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki); [Ye et al., 2023 benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs-vs-sm2); [Anki Manual: Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)).

**What to do:** Put chord symbols, spellings, key signatures, and scale degrees into Anki. Show up and answer the daily queue.

## Drill With a Timer

Flashcards build knowing; drills build playing. Procedural memory needs physical repetition, feedback, and time pressure. The strongest predictor of musical skill level is not total time with the instrument — it is focused, goal-directed solitary practice ([Ericsson, Krampe, & Tesch-Römer, 1993](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/18468/excerpt/9780521518468_excerpt.pdf)).

**What to do:** Use the chord drill with a timer. Lift your hands, place the voicing, and beat the clock.

## Woodshed in All 12 Keys

Jazz pianists take one small idea — a voicing, a lick, a scale pattern — and work it through every key with a metronome until it is automatic ([Free Jazz Lessons: Barry Harris](https://www.freejazzlessons.com/barry-harris/); [PianoGroove: Barry Harris Voicings](https://www.pianogroove.com/jazz-piano-lessons/barry-harris-voicings/); [JEN: Woodshedding](https://jazzednet.org/resources/a-structured-approach-to-woodshedding-improvisation/)). Resources like Mark Levine's *The Jazz Piano Book* and *The Drop 2 Book* catalog the standard vocabulary ([Sher Music: The Jazz Piano Book](https://www.long-mcquade.com/233176/Print-Music/Piano/Sher-Music-Company/The-Jazz-Piano-Book-Levine-Piano-Book.htm); [Sher Music: The Drop 2 Book](https://www.shermusic.com/products/jazz-piano-masterclass-with-mark-levine-the-drop-2-book)).

**What to do:** Pick one voicing per week. Play it through all twelve keys with a metronome.

## Mix It Up and Rest

Interleaving — alternating chord drills, scales, and repertoire in one session — improves long-term retention and transfer, even though it feels harder in the moment ([Rohrer & Taylor, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8); [Carvalho & Goldstone, 2015](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-014-0676-4)). Intense focus should be followed by diffuse-mode rest; consolidation happens during breaks and sleep ([Oakley & Sejnowski, 2018](https://barbaraoakley.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/10-Top-Ideas-to-Help-Your-Learning-and-10-Pitfalls-1.pdf)).

**What to do:** Alternate activities inside a session. Then walk away.

## The Simple Weekly Loop

1. **Anki** — daily retrieval of symbols, spellings, and scale degrees.
2. **Timed drill** — physical execution of what Anki selected.
3. **Woodshedding** — one voicing through all twelve keys with a metronome.
4. **Repertoire** — apply the material to real music.
5. **Rest** — sleep and breaks are part of practice.

## Three Mistakes to Avoid

- **Passive review.** Highlighting and re-reading feel productive but rank as "low utility" learning techniques, while practice testing and distributed practice are the only "high utility" techniques in a major review ([Dunlosky et al., 2013](http://lexiconic.net/pedagogy/Improving_Students_Learning.pdf)).
- **Cramming.** The same total study time produces better long-term retention when spread across sessions than when massed into one ([Cepeda et al., 2008](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0kp5q19x/qt0kp5q19x.pdf)).
- **Practicing only the theory.** Knowing the symbol is not the same as playing the chord ([Cohen & Squire, 1980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4464729/)).
